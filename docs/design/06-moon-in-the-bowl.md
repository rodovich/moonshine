# Slice 6 — Moon in the Bowl: the Climax and the Ending

This is the build-ready design for Act 6, the last act ([overview](overview.md), [story bible](../story-bible.md)). It follows straight on from the **Moonrise.** card at the end of [05-high-and-dry.md](05-high-and-dry.md): Cheryl sitting on a coil of chain in the Windlass Room, the bell's old clapper seated where the Tooth was, waiting for the bell.

## The slice in brief

The bell rings at the turn. Cheryl throws the brake, and the tide hauls Tessel home: the chain comes in link by link, each one a thunderclap, while all over the city the Dry Canal Day crowd falls over, the piano rolls into the Boatman's boat, the Old Man's chess pieces fly, and from the lighthouse the Teeth slide away into the dark. The sealed hatch opens onto the **Long Keel**, the straight spine of the hull, and its lamps wake ahead of her all the way to the **Sighting Room** under Cistern Court.

Moonlight falls down a shaft onto a dry stone bowl big enough to sit in, and at the bottom of the bowl is her bath plug. *My plug.* It fits. The Basin fills. In the water: the Gate, and the moon, off to one side. She turns the trim wheel until the moon sits true (two fingers past the Gate, two nights past full, as the Keepers' log says), and the Basin's rim lights, and every lamp Downstairs blazes. The Keepers' master stop comes free: *put her back to bed.* The Middle Sister goes quiet, the city settles to its proper waterline, and far above, the canals come rushing back. "I'M AFLOAT! AND I'VE GOT A PIANO!"

Cheryl takes off her shoes and gets into the Basin. The best bath of her life, at the bottom of the city, with her own plug, the moon in the water around her. Nobody will ever know.

At dawn she rides the Keepers' lift up and climbs out of the well in Cistern Court. The Old Man looks up from his chess: "Morning." Then a last walk through a town explaining the night to itself, wrongly, in a dozen different ways: the Mayor's clock, Ottilie's bootleggers, a seiche, the Pelletier boys. The Stair Race. The ferry docking first time. The piano, delivered at last, "from Gerald." And at her kitchen table, a tick on the moon chart, in the Gate. It isn't Moonshine Night. *But it counts.* Then a year later, on the next Moonshine Night: Cheryl in her own tub on the roof, with a new plug, and the moon rising dead in the Gate.

**Playtime:** 15–25 minutes (the epilogue walk is as long as the player likes). **Maps:** 2 new (Long Keel, Sighting Room), plus night states for the montage and morning states for the whole town. **New items:** the bath plug (found, then left behind). **Wrongness:** 0.5, falling to 0 the moment the moon sits true.

## The climax

### The bell

Control returns after the Moonrise card, with Cheryl on the chain coil.

- **The speaking tube** (15, 2): far off, through the tube: the Dry Canal Day crowd, music (an accordion), and the Mayor (FAR-OFF VOICE: …citizens… …the canals are a FEATURE…).
- **The brake lever** (17, 8), before the bell: "Locked. 'When the bell says when.'"
- **The bell** fires the first time Cheryl touches the brake lever, or after 15 seconds of the player doing anything else, whichever comes first → `bell.final`:
  1. Cut (no fade) to the **belfry at night**. Ambrose at the rope, eyes shut, moonlight on his face. Hold two seconds.
  2. AMBROSE: THE TURN. THE BIG ONE.
  3. AMBROSE: …NOW.
  4. Cut back to the Windlass Room. Through the speaking tube, three great strokes of the bell, each with a 1 px shake. On the first stroke, the brake lever unlocks with a loud *clack*.
  5. "The bell says when."
- Control returns. The brake now works.

### The hauling

**Trigger:** Cheryl throws the brake → `brake.thrown`. Input is locked until the end of the montage.

1. The brake swings over. A deep shudder. Far off, through the walls, the Tide Wheel bites (a huge, slow groan rising to a roar). The drum begins to turn, slowly, the wrong way first (the chain pays out a link; Cheryl staggers), then the clapper *clacks* into the drum's teeth and holds, and the drum turns the right way.
2. The chain comes in through the hawse, link by link. Each link arriving is a thunderclap: a deep metal BOOM, a 2 px shake, and the clapper's ratchet *clack* after it. They come every 1.5 seconds, then faster.
3. Internal monologue:
   - *Wind her in slowly.*
   - *And home again.*
4. **The montage** (instant cuts, 2–3 seconds each; the chain's booms continue under all of them; auto boxes at 1.2 seconds):
   1. **`bilge_dry`, Dry Canal Day.** The Mayor on the bridge, lantern raised. MAYOR: …and so, citizens, tonight Tessel steps FORWARD— → a lurch (3 px), and the whole crowd sits down at once (a falling-over pose). The piano rolls along the mud, gathering speed, and drops into the Boatman's boat with a crash of chords. BOATMAN: …I've got a piano.
   2. **Cistern Court.** The lurch. The Old Man's chess pieces fly off the board. OLD MAN: GERALD! On the roof, the Neighbor's sheets stand straight out like flags.
   3. **Lamp room.** The Keeper at the window. Through it, the Teeth (stage 3) slide slowly away to one side and shrink into the dark. LIGHTHOUSE KEEPER: It's going. It's GOING.
   4. **Risen Harbor.** Mott at the quay edge. Low over the eastern Rim, the moon, just risen. He raises a mug of tea to it. No line.
   5. **Belfry.** Ambrose still holding the rope, eyes shut. AMBROSE: HOME AGAIN.
5. Back to the Windlass Room. The booms slow. The last link comes in. The drum stops. Silence, except for the sea through the hawse, which is calmer now. → `chain.home`.
6. In the north wall, the moon-in-bowl emblem on the hatch lights cyan, and the hatch grinds open (7–8, 1–2).
7. "It's stopped. We're home. Or near enough."
8. "Now the moon in the bowl."
9. Control returns.

**Afterward, in the Windlass Room:** the drum: "Still. Holding. The clapper's doing a job it never knew it had." The brake: "Thrown. I'm leaving it thrown." The hawse: "The sea's quieter. It's had its say." The speaking tube: "Somebody up there is laughing. A lot of people are laughing. And somebody's crying about a piano."

### The Sighting Room: the plug

**On first entering:** the music falls away to silence. A column of silver moonlight falls down the shaft onto the Basin. Something small glints at the bottom of the bowl.

- **The Basin, dry, with the plug** (first look, from anywhere next to it):
  1. Internal monologue: *My plug.*
  2. "My plug. Chain and all. It went down my drain on Moonshine Night and fell all the way here." / "Of course it did. Everything in this town goes down eventually."
  3. → take → `got.plug`; *Got Bath Plug.*
- **The Basin, dry, without the plug**: "A stone bowl, big enough to sit in, at the bottom of the shaft. A plughole in the middle, and no stopper. Long gone."
- **Plug used on the Basin** → `basin.plugged`:
  1. "The Basin's own stopper went missing centuries ago. Mine fits." / "Plugs are plugs."
  2. Water rises through a grille in the Basin's floor, slow and clean, filling it over four seconds (animated). The moonlight lands on the water and breaks into a hundred pieces, then settles.
  3. **The Basin view** (full-screen picture): a circle of still water, framed by the stone rim, reflecting what the mirror at the top of the shaft sees: the dark Rim, the notch of the Gate, and the moon, off to the right of it.
  4. "The moon, in the bowl. And the Gate. And the moon's not in the Gate." / "Moon in the Gate and moon in the bowl."
- **The shaft** (looking up from beside the Basin): "Straight up. All the way up to my roof. To my tub." / "I've been bathing on top of this for thirty-one years."

### The Sighting Room: the trim

- **The trim wheel** (3–4, 1–2), before the Basin is full: "A bronze wheel, marked with a moon and an arrow. It won't turn. It wants something to look at first."
- **The trim wheel**, once the Basin is full: each interaction turns it one step (a deep creak, a 1 px shake as the whole hull swings a hair on its chain), then shows the Basin view with the moon moved, then a description:
  - **Step 1:** the moon slides left, toward the Gate. "Closer."
  - **Step 2:** the moon sits dead in the middle of the Gate. "Dead in the Gate. Like every Moonshine Night of my life." / "But it isn't Moonshine Night. It's two nights past full. 'Allow two fingers.'"
  - **Step 3:** the moon slides on, two fingers past the middle → `moon.true`. See "Moon true."
  - Once true, the wheel won't turn further: "It won't go any further. It knows."
- **The sighting log** (14, 4): a stone stand with a bronze-bound book. Each look reads one entry, newest first:
  1. "'Moon true in the bowl.' Signed with a B. The last entry. Three hundred years ago."
  2. "'Past full: allow two fingers. Past half: allow a hand.' A rule, written large, for anyone who comes after."
  3. "Pages of it. 'Moon true. Moon true. A finger west; corrected. Moon true.'"
  - After `moon.true`, use (or interact again) → `log.signed`: "There's a stub of pencil in the stand. Pencils keep." / "I write under his: 'Rang at the turn. Moon true in the bowl. All well.' And a C." / "Somebody should."

### Moon true

**Trigger:** `trim` reaches 3.

1. The Basin view holds. The moon settles two fingers past the Gate, and the water goes perfectly still.
2. The Basin's moonglass rim lights, from one point all the way round, cyan.
3. Cut back to the room: a soft, rising chime, and the lamps all along the walls flare to full brightness. Far off down the Long Keel, lamp after lamp blazes (heard as a run of chimes going away).
4. Wrongness goes to 0. The music, for the first time in the game, is entirely in tune.
5. Beside the Basin, the master stop (9, 6) unlocks with a *clack* and rises an inch.
6. "Moon in the Gate, and moon in the bowl."
7. "And the greedy one. Put her back to bed."

### The master stop and the refill

- **The master stop**, before `moon.true`: "A bronze rod in the floor, with the three-bars emblem, and the middle bar picked out. Locked."
- **The master stop**, after: pull → `sisters.asleep`:
  1. Far off, a cough, and a thump that stops. Then silence.
  2. The whole room eases down, gently (a 1 px tilt that settles to level over two seconds).
  3. "There. Asleep." / "And we're settling. Down to where we belong."
  4. **The refill montage** (instant cuts, auto boxes):
     1. **The Bilge, night.** Water rushing back into the canals from the canal mouth, foaming along the trench, rising. The Boatman's boat lifts off the mud with the piano in it. BOATMAN: I'M AFLOAT! I'M AFLOAT AND I'VE GOT A PIANO!
     2. The crowd on the quays, soaked by the rush, cheering. FISHMONGER: It came BACK! I KNEW it would!
     3. MAYOR: Dry Canal Day is… postponed!
  5. Back to the Sighting Room. Quiet. The rim glowing; the moon in the water.

## The bath

**Trigger:** interacting with the Basin after `sisters.asleep`.

1. "The best bath I'll ever have, and I didn't bring a towel."
2. Cheryl takes off her shoes and sets them side by side beside the Basin (a shoes sprite at (6, 6)). She climbs in and sits, in her clothes (she hadn't planned a bath; her striped costume is at home, which is the joke of "I didn't bring a towel"), facing down, the moonlight on her. The sitting-in-water sprite and the Basin view's reflection show her ordinary cardigan and bun, not the costume.
3. The music box plays the skipping rhyme, slowly, perfectly in tune: the tune from the Moonrise scene in Act 1, this time not going flat.
4. **The Basin view** once more, now with Cheryl's reflection at the edge of the circle: the Gate, the moon true, the ripples from her settling.
5. Internal monologue (the game's last):
   - *The best bath of my life is in a stone bowl at the bottom of the city, with my own plug.*
   - *Nobody will ever know.*
   - *Most of the best things, nobody ever knows.*
6. Hold on the view for three seconds after the last line. Fade to black. Card: **Later.**
7. Fade in: Cheryl standing by the Basin, shoes on, hair a little damp. → `bathed`. The Basin stays full, the plug in it.

**Afterward:**
- **The Basin**: "Full, still, with the moon in it. My plug in the bottom." / "I'm leaving it. It's the Basin's plug now. Moon in the bowl."
- **Trying to take the plug back**: "No. It belongs here. I'll find another one."
- **The lift** (6–7, 1–2): "A bronze cage in a shaft of its own, with a counterweight. 'Up,' says the arrow." → choice "Go up?" — **Yes** / **Not yet**. Yes → the dawn.

## The dawn

1. Cheryl steps into the cage; the gate closes. A clank, the long rumble of the counterweight going down as she goes up, and darkness with a slow upward scroll of shaft wall lit by her disc (about four seconds).
2. Cut to **Cistern Court at dawn**: pale pink and gold. The iron grate on the well lifts, and Cheryl climbs out (a climbing-out sprite) and stands at (9, 18).
3. The Old Man at his chess table looks up.
4. OLD MAN: Morning.
5. Hold one second.
6. OLD MAN: Gerald says you came out of the well. I said, don't be ridiculous, Gerald.
7. "Morning." (Cheryl's description, to herself.) Then: "I should go and see the town. Then I've a tick to make."
8. Control returns. → `epilogue`. Music: `neighborhood`, bright and entirely in tune, for the first time with its full arrangement since Act 1.

## Decisions

- **Act 6 begins with the clapper already seated** (moved to the end of Act 5), so the climax starts with the bell.
- **The bell comes on its own if the player waits,** so the climax can't stall.
- **The montage is two short sets of instant cuts** (the lurch and the refill), showing the whole cast without leaving the underground for long.
- **The piano lands in the Boatman's boat during the lurch, and he delivers it by boat.** It resolves the piano's journey, and his.
- **Cheryl leaves her plug in the Basin.** It's the Basin's plug now; the Shopkeeper gives her a new one, his first "sale," if she visits.
- **The trim targets "two fingers past" the Gate, not the middle,** because the climax is two nights after full moon (Moonshine Night is night 0; Acts 2–3 are day 1; Acts 4–5 are day 2; Act 6 is that night); the Keepers' rule ("Past full: allow two fingers") was planted in Act 3 and is repeated in the sighting log here.
- **The Brannoc grace note is in the sighting log,** which is in the Sighting Room itself, so the player doesn't have to detour. Cheryl signs "C." under his "B." Optional, never pointed out.
- **Ambrose's epilogue line is the last Keeper's last entry, word for word: "RANG AT THE TURN. ALL WELL."** Neither of them knows.
- **The epilogue is a free walk with one gentle gate** (leave the court once), so players can linger or go straight home.
- **The final image is the Act 1 vista, made right, a year later on the next Moonshine Night,** with Cheryl in her tub. The moon is dead in the Gate because it's Moonshine Night again; on the climax night it was correctly two fingers past. (From review by xaKh8TS_PJ.)
- **Revised after review:** the bell got a steel clapper when the clock was fitted (Act 4), so it can ring without its old bronze one; Ambrose points at the doorstop; Mott toasts the moon with tea, since his jug is a doorstop; the climax is two nights past full; the sighting log has a pencil, not wet ink; the piano arrives flat and Cheryl will tune it; the Shopkeeper heard about the plug from the Old Man.
- **The credits end with "No one ever knew."**
