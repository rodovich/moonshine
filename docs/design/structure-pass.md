# Structure pass: Acts 2 to 5 (proposal)

This is a proposal for review, not yet the design. Once the reviewer and the orchestrator approve it, its decisions move into [02-city.md](02-city.md) through [05-high-and-dry.md](05-high-and-dry.md) and the [overview](overview.md), and the builders get their specs.

## The proposal in brief

The client played to about Act 4 and found the game thinner and more linear than intended (their notes are quoted in full at the end). The cause is mostly Act 2: it has two threads, one of which is "give someone your hat," and most of the people the player meets there have nothing to do until Act 4 or 5. The fixes:

1. **Act 2 gets three required threads instead of two,** and five more characters get jobs in them. The Counting Woman now needs to see the "ninety-nine" in print *twice* before she storms off. One printed source is the guidebook, which now comes through a chain of trades between characters (the Lighthouse Keeper's telescope, the Shopkeeper's window, the Tourist) instead of Cheryl's own hat. The other is the Tide-Sheet, the newspaper on Lamp Street, which the Man on Bench can't read because his wife is wearing his glasses in her hair.
2. **The telescope comes back in Act 5.** The Lighthouse Keeper gave it away because it kept pointing at the sea. After the reveal, she wants it back to watch the reef, and she lets Cheryl use the lamp in return. This replaces the brass polish and gives her a small arc across the game.
3. **Ottilie becomes the hub of Act 4.** Reading the Ship Plan, she now points at three concrete places: the Fountain, Mott's copper, and the Skipping Girls.
4. **Signposting:** a staged beat and a sound mark the hollow step once the Counting Woman leaves; the Square gets new lines that point back to it; the paper and charcoal come with clear instructions and a reply for everything that can't be rubbed; the Harbor is reached by walking through the Bilge.
5. **Geography:** the Goat Path becomes a natural cliff path three screens tall, the same height as the Hundred Steps, and it comes down the west side of the island (the Back) into the west end of the Bilge, not into the Harbor. The Harbor stays east of the Bilge, so the map's left and right agree everywhere.

Everything else stands: Act 1 (apart from the hat), Act 3's shape, Act 4's three threads, Act 5's three threads, and Act 6.

## 5. Geography

### The rule

The story bible already sets it: north is the Crown, south is the Harbor, **the Back is the steep west end**, and the Prow (the lighthouse) points southeast. The Goat Path's top is right (it leaves Lamp Street by the west gate), but its bottom isn't: it comes out at the Harbor, which is east of the Bilge. The proposal moves the bottom, not the top, because the west side also has Mott's shack and the sea cave, and the Goat Path's own lines already say "You can't see the Gate from here" (the Gate is east).

### The Goat Path, rebuilt

- **16 × 42 tiles, three screens tall**, the same height as the Hundred Steps.
- **A natural cliff path:** bare rock, scree, gorse and wild thyme, goat-worn ground, a few rough cut steps where it's steep, and a weathered post-and-rope rail on the drop side. No garden beds; the only built things are Mott's shack and its yard wall.
- **Switchbacks:** five legs, each running the full width of the map, with hairpin turns. The walk is longer than the Steps, which is the point.
- **Height cues:** at the top, gulls wheel *below* the path and the lower town's roofs are small at the east edge; halfway, a viewpoint at a hairpin looks out west over the open Bowl to the western Rim; near the bottom, the roofs are at eye level and the sea is close. Cheryl's first line at the top: "The Goat Path. Down the back of the island the long way. Same drop as the Steps, plus goats."
- **On the way:** Mott's shack on the third leg (its Act 4 state stays as designed); the sea cave mouth near the bottom, at the waterline (the Act 4 Sea Cave map is unchanged).
- **The bottom** leaves by the east edge onto the **west end of the Bilge's south quay** (the flooded paving in Act 2), arriving at (1, 12) facing right.

## 9. What isn't changing, and why

- **Act 1**, apart from the hat. It's built, polished, and tested; its two "give" puzzles are its tutorial.
- **The soap on the piano.** It's set up in Act 1, it's Cheryl using a thing rather than handing it over, and it's the funniest set piece in Act 2.
- **The moon chart to Mott.** It's evidence, not a payment, and it's the moment Mott stops drinking.
- **Act 3's single thread at the start, and Act 6's linear climax.** Both are short and deliberate.
- **The Pump Hall and the Windlass bottlenecks.** Each closes an act on a payoff.
