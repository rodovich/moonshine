# Slice 4 — The List: Waking the Sisters, and the Sea Going Out

This is the build-ready design for Act 4 ([overview](overview.md), [story bible](../story-bible.md)). It picks up the morning after [03-downstairs.md](03-downstairs.md) ends, with the Fountain stopped and the doorsteps glowing at night. It ends with the act's big moment and the game's changed-surface reveal: Cheryl climbs out of the Fishmonger's ice cellar into a Bilge where every canal has run dry, because she made the city rise six feet out of the sea.

> **Structure pass (client feedback).** [structure-pass.md](structure-pass.md) adds to this act: Ottilie's reading of the Ship Plan names four leads (the bell, Mott's copper, the Fountain, the Girls; see "Ottilie Brask" below); the Man on Bridge is the Man on Bench (on his bench on Lamp Street until the reveal, then on the Bilge bridge); the Shop window shows the Keeper's telescope, and the Tourist wears the Shop's straw hat; the Goat Path is rebuilt, with Mott's shack and the sea cave on the new path; and the Bilge has a west exit to the Goat Path (on `bilge_dry`, (0, 12–14); like the other exits, walking into it after the reveal ends the act). **From Act 4 on, the hundredth step is boarded over** (client feedback: no refusals that gate nothing): a plank lid with a painted MIND THE STEP, walkable, with the step's "bonk". Look: "Somebody's boarded over the hundredth step. MIND THE STEP. BY ORDER." / "The Mayor's handwriting. He's never forgiven a step." This replaces the open hole and its "Not now" line in Acts 4–6; nothing Downstairs is needed by that route after Act 3. The Tide Wheel stays reachable after the burst (as built; nothing gains by closing it). The Shopkeeper declines the guidebook in this act ("My window is complete."), because the Clockmaker needs it. The Man on Bench, with his glasses on, has the 04 first line, then alternates (as built): "New Tide-Sheet. Glasses on. It says the Bilge is 'a little damp.' I can read every word. I liked it better blurred." / "Low tide 'a little lower than usual,' it says. Printed. In ink. I can read every word of it."

## The slice in brief

Tessel is listing. The Bilge floods a little deeper every time Cheryl looks, the piano has slid off the Fountain into a canal, and the town has decided this all started when the Fountain stopped, which Cheryl knows she did. Her rubbing of the Chart Room floor shows three bars low in the hull, where the water is, and a rope from the bell going down to a wheel. *Pumps. And something to drive them. And Ambrose's bell.*

Three threads, in any order:

- **The bell.** The Clockmaker has barricaded himself in the belfry with his perfect clock. The guidebook says Tessel keeps its own time, "eleven minutes behind the mainland," and he can't bear to see *his* eleven in print. He runs to the sundial, leaving the tower open. In the belfry, a lever marked DO NOT TOUCH lifts the clock's drive off the old bell rope. Ambrose climbs the stairs, waits for the turn of the tide, and rings. Under everything, something enormous begins to turn. The Mayor storms up from the Square to complain about a bell at TWENTY PAST, leaving the cordon around the dry Fountain unguarded. Ambrose shows Cheryl the flyleaf of his grandmother's ledger: the true rhyme.
- **The copper throat.** Captain Mott hasn't had a drink since the doorsteps lit up, and he's frightened. Cheryl shows him her moon chart; it's the first proof that someone else has seen what he's seen. He gives her the copper coil from his still, which his grandfather pulled out of the sea cave ("off the greedy one, Grandad said"), and his square jug.
- **The order.** The Skipping Girls each know the rhyme "properly," three different ways. Ottilie reads the glyphs on the Ship Plan's three bars as "Thin, Stout, and Middle: types of eel, obviously." Together they give the order: Thin, Stout, Middle. They also carry a warning that Cheryl doesn't understand: *Middle sister's greedy, put her back to bed, or up jumps Tessel and bumps her head.*

Then down through the dry Fountain's hatch to the **Pump Hall**. Cheryl fits Mott's coil into the Middle Sister and wakes the three pumps in order. They cough to life one after another, like old women at a funeral. The floor levels. The list is fixed. She turns to put the Middle Sister back to bed, and the valve won't close: the coil has spent forty years making moonshine and has forgotten how to stop. The floor keeps rising. Her ears pop. A ballast cell bursts and water floods the way she came in. The only way out is an emergency ladder going up, and up, while the roar below fades into silence and then into gulls that are much too close. She shoulders open a hatch into the Fishmonger's ice cellar, climbs the stairs, and opens the door.

**Every canal in the Bilge is empty.** Boats lie on their sides in green mud. Crabs pick across the canal beds. The Boatman sits upright in his boat on dry ground. The piano rests on the mud. The canal walls below the old waterline aren't stone at all, but dark, fitted Founder slate, crusted with barnacles. At the foot of the Water Stairs, where no one has ever seen, is a bronze door. Tessel thinks the sea has gone out. The sea didn't go anywhere. *We came up out of it. I did that.*

**Playtime:** 35–50 minutes. **Maps:** 6 new (Belfry, Pump Hall, Tide Wheel, Emergency Shaft, Ice Cellar, Sea Cave), plus new states of Cistern Court, Lamp Street, the Crown, the Hundred Steps, Fountain Square, the Bilge (three flood stages and the dry reveal), the Goat Path, and the Harbor. **NPCs:** returning cast, plus the Clockmaker in person and two bystanders for the reveal. **New items:** copper throat, square jug.

## Building up to the reveal

The brief asks that the changed surface be built up to, revealed excitingly, significant, and make the player feel they've made things worse. This act is designed around that. The rules for everyone building it:

1. **The list gets visibly worse while the player works.** The Bilge has three flood stages (see State). Each time Cheryl comes back, more of it is under water, the Fishmonger has retreated further, and the piano has slid further into the canal. Small things slide on every map. The town blames the Fountain, which Cheryl stopped. She is fixing a problem everyone thinks she caused.
2. **Every thread drops the same warning, and nobody explains it.** The Third Girl's favorite line ("up jumps Tessel and bumps her head!"); Mott's grandfather ("off the greedy one"); Ambrose's flyleaf; Cheryl's own Act 3 thought about "a narrow house." The player should arrive at the Pump Hall half-knowing that the Middle Sister is trouble, and still have no choice but to wake her.
3. **The fix works first.** The Sisters start and the floor levels. Let the player feel relief for a few seconds before it goes wrong.
4. **The going-wrong is felt, not shown.** Underground, the player can't see the city rise. They feel it: the floor tilting the other way, the cell windows draining, ears popping, a deep groan through the whole hull, then the burst and the flood.
5. **The climb is a long, deliberate breath.** Three screens of ladder. The sound changes as Cheryl goes up. The player knows something has happened and doesn't know what.
6. **The reveal is shown, slowly, then made funny, then made personal.** First a silent pan across the dry Bilge, ending on the bronze door. Then the town's wrong explanations. Then Cheryl's own short monologue: *I did that.*
7. **It must change play.** The dry canals and the exposed door are where Act 5 happens. The Fountain refills over its hatch, so the Pump Hall can't be reached again.

### Engine calls for this act

All on engine/core (186be2c):
- The hull leveling and over-tilting: `g.tilt(dx, dy, seconds)` eases to a held offset; `g.tilt(0, 0, seconds)` eases back; shakes add on top.
- Stepping out into sunlight: `g.fade('out', seconds, 'white_light')`, then `g.fade('in', seconds)`. Use a palette light color, never pure white.
- Spreading floodwater and draining grates: a loop of `g.setTile(layer, x, y, value)` and `g.wait(0.15)`. For changes that last (the dry Bilge, the refilled Fountain), use the map's `patches(g)` keyed to a flag.
- The reveal's pan: chain `await g.panCamera(x, y, 0.5)` legs (east along the canal, then down the Water Stairs to the door), hold, then `g.followPlayer(speed)`.

- **The teacup** (on the table, new entity at (5, 4)): "My teacup, at the very edge of the table. Downhill. The table isn't downhill." / Then: "I've moved it back to the middle three times. It keeps going."

**The Ship Plan, first look in Act 4** (after the picture closes, once only; descriptions, not monologue):
- "Three bars, low in the hull, down where the water is. They're pumps. They must be pumps."
- "And a rope from the bell, down to a wheel. If the bell turns the wheel, and the wheel drives the pumps…"

**Internal monologue in this act** is kept to three moments (story bible budget): the flyleaf ("It's instructions"), the escape ("Up, then"), and the reveal. Everything else is description.

If the player doesn't look at the Ship Plan, Ambrose, Mott, and Ottilie each point at the same three things (see their dialog).

## Waking the Sisters

**Requires:** `pump.throat` and `bell.restored`. A wake-valve opened before the throat is fitted turns, and the Sister shivers but doesn't start: "She shivers and goes still. She needs her throat." (No reset needed.)

- Thin: "She coughs, and catches, and goes." Stout: "And the stout one. Thump. Thump." Middle: see below.

A wrong valve at any point:
- Every Sister that was running coughs and stops; all valves spin shut. `pump.order` resets to 0.
- First time: "They cough, and stop, like a class that's been asked a question." Second time: "Wrong order. They know it, even if I don't." Later times: "No."

**The third valve, the Middle Sister** → `pumps.running`. The rise sequence begins.

## The rise

Input is locked until step 12, except where noted. All shakes are 1 px unless noted.

1. The Middle Sister coughs, catches, and goes. All three are running together. The thump is now a rolling, three-part rhythm. Music: the **pumps** cue (see Audio) starts under it.
2. The cell grates: the water below them begins to drop, visibly, tile by tile, over four seconds.
3. A long, low creak runs through the whole hull, and the screen eases one pixel back toward level (a slow 2-second shake in one direction).
4. "The floor's levelling. I can feel it through my shoes."
5. "It worked. It's working." (Then hold three seconds with no text. Let this be relief.)
6. "Middle sister's greedy. Put her back to bed."
7. Control returns briefly: Cheryl must walk to the Middle Sister's valve (16, 5) and use it (the only interaction that does anything; others get "Not now."). → She hauls on it. It won't turn. A hiss, and a sweet, sharp smell.
8. "It won't close. It smells like Mott's shack." / "The coil's stuck open. Forty years of making moonshine. It's forgotten how to stop."
9. The creak again, longer, and this time the screen tilts the *other* way and keeps going (`g.tilt`, 2 px, held three seconds). Cheryl staggers east with the tilt to (20, 6) (scripted, three steps). The cell grates are now dry. The Sisters keep thumping. The music's pitch begins to rise very slightly.
10. Cheryl's ears pop: a short, high, muffled tone, and all sound dips for half a second. "My ears just popped. Underground. That's not right."
11. A tremendous groan through the hull; a 3 px shake. Then a crack and a roar from the west: **the big main along the west wall splits.** The Sisters push the ballast water out through that main, and its usual outlet, the Fountain's feed, is the one Cheryl jammed shut in Act 3. With three pumps running and nowhere to go, the pressure bursts the pipe. Water sprays from the split and spreads across the floor toward the ladder to the Fountain, covering the west end tile by tile over six seconds, and is forced up the Fountain's shaft (which is how the Fountain refills over its hatch). ## Decisions

- **Act 4 runs from the morning after Act 3 to the reveal,** with three parallel threads. The bell thread opens the way down; the other two are free in order.
- **The guidebook's "eleven minutes behind" entry is the key to the belfry.** It was planted in Act 2 and pays off the Clockmaker's obsession with his eleven minutes.
- **Ambrose rings, not Cheryl.** She can pull the rope herself, but it only clangs: the bell is for the turn, and only a Tull knows when that is. It keeps the residents unknowingly saving the city.
- **The order puzzle uses three wrong versions of one rhyme plus Ottilie's eel names.** The true version, from Ambrose, arrives after the bell as confirmation and plants the Act 5–6 verses.
- **The Sisters stand on the floor in a different order (Stout, Thin, Middle) from their waking order,** so walking left to right isn't the answer.
- **The Middle Sister fails because the substitute part is Mott's still coil.** The fix that seemed clever causes the rise; the "moonshine" pun pays off at the worst moment.
- **The flood never catches Cheryl.** It creates urgency without a fail state (design rule 4).
- **The reveal is a silent pan ending on the bronze door, then the town's wrong explanations, then three lines of monologue ending "I did that."** Exciting, then funny, then personal.
- **The dry Bilge exposes the city's Founder hull** (slate below the old waterline): the first time the hidden city is visible from the street, and the clearest proof yet that Tessel floats.
- **The Water Stairs' last flight is broken,** so the bronze door is visible but unreachable until Act 5.
- **The Counting Woman counts one hundred in Act 4,** as soon as the step is open, rather than waiting for the epilogue. The epilogue can still give her a final moment.
- **Revised after review (xaKh8TS_PJ):** the flood stops west of Cheryl (she staggers east with the tilt); the bronze door is reachable only from the mud, since the stairs stop above a broken flight; the burst is the main pipe, overpressured because Cheryl jammed the Fountain's feed in Act 3, which also refills the Fountain; the free roam after the reveal stays in the Bilge; the flyleaf's pump names are under a tea stain, so the order thread still matters; Mott can be found and asks for written proof; one sincere beat (the Boatman's drying planks); the glare reveal and the missing lapping; the trickle to the lower Bowl and the streaming laundry.
- **"Brenda" and "Albert" are named once each, in passing,** by each other. Their labels stay descriptive (FISHMONGER, MAN ON BRIDGE).
